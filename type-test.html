Editorial Playfair vs Retail DM Sans on product names.

Three identical surfaces (PLP card grid · PDP buy box · HOF editorial hero) shown side by side. Variant A keeps Playfair on product names — the current "editorial / Apple-quiet" approach. Variant B switches product names to DM Sans — the "retail / Sweetwater" approach. Body, prices, and buttons stay identical in both.

Locked rules either way — Pricing & numbers always DM Sans 500. Buttons always DM Sans 600 uppercase. Eyebrows always DM Sans uppercase 12px. The only thing that moves is the product name itself.

Variant A — Editorial (current)

Playfair Display on product names + section titles. DM Sans on body, prices, buttons. Brand voice = pro-audio heritage, story-led, "this gear has history."

Variant B — Retail (proposed)

DM Sans on product names + body + prices + buttons. Playfair only on H1 / hero / HOF article titles. Brand voice = clean, fast, scannable, conversion-led.

How to decide — quick rule of thumb

If conversion > brand voice: pick Variant B (retail / DM Sans on names). Sweetwater, Sweetwater, Reverb, Thomann all do this. Easier to scan. Faster decisions. Better for paid traffic.

If brand voice > conversion lift on PLP: pick Variant A (editorial / Playfair on names). What you have today. Stronger differentiation. Pro-audio heritage feel. Closer to Apple / NYT product pages.

Hybrid (AJ's recommendation): Variant B on PLP and PDP (where conversion lives) + Variant A on HOF, gear heritage articles, About, and program landing pages (where story lives). Best of both — and matches the spec rule that numbers and prices are always DM Sans 500.
